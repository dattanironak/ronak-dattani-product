CASE STUDY · INDEPENDENT PRODUCT PROJECT

QSR Voice AI Agent

A voice-first ordering system for quick-service restaurants. Customers speak their order at the counter, watch the cart build on screen, confirm a read-back and pay by scanning a UPI QR code.

SpeakCustomer orders by voice
→
UnderstandSTT → LLM, grounded in the live menu
→
BuildCart updates live on screen
→
ConfirmAgent reads order back
→
PayScan UPI QR, get pickup code

01Business context

Quick-service restaurants live on throughput. At peak hours the bottleneck is the counter: one staff member taking orders, answering menu questions and handling payment. Voice AI is now good enough to take part of that load, if it can be trusted with real orders and real money.

02Customer problem

  • Customers wait in line, get rushed through a menu with many customisations, and sometimes get the wrong order.
  • Staff repeat the same menu answers all day and reconcile payments by hand.
  • Owners need menu changes and order history without calling a developer.

03My role

Independent product project: I defined the core experience, set scope, and built the whole system end to end. That covers the voice agent, the ordering and payment backend, and the customer and admin web apps.

04Discovery

Walked through a counter order step by step, from greeting to pickup, and noted where it slows down or goes wrong: unclear menu questions, forgotten required options, misheard items, and payment hand-offs. Each failure point became a product rule for the agent.

05Prioritisation

MUSTVoice ordering grounded in the real menu, a live on-screen cart, order read-back, UPI payment
SHOULDAdmin dashboard for menu management, role-based access, conversation logs for debugging
LATERReceipt printer (a printable on-screen receipt stands in for now), more payment methods, in-app admin sign-up

06Product decisions

  • Never invent menu items. The agent can only mention items, prices and options that exist in the live menu, and checks item details through a tool before confirming.
  • Designed for speech, not chat. Short replies, one question at a time, no reading out long lists. It asks "burgers, wraps or drinks?" instead of listing everything.
  • Read-back before commit. The agent summarises items and total and waits for a yes before creating the order. A guard prevents duplicate orders.
  • Screen and voice stay in sync. Every cart change is pushed to the screen, so the customer can see what the agent heard.
  • UPI only. A single payment path that suits Indian counter checkout, with a staff fallback if payment fails.
  • Least-privilege admin. Owners manage the menu and full history. Treasurers see only today's orders.

07Execution

Three parts: a LiveKit voice agent (speech-to-text, LLM and text-to-speech), a FastAPI backend with Postgres for menu, orders and PayU payments, and a React web app for customers and admins. Conversations are recorded for debugging, built so a recording failure can never break a live call.

08Success metrics

A working end-to-end prototype: voice order to paid order with pickup code. For a pilot, these are the metrics I'd track:

  • Order completion rate: calls that end in a paid order
  • Order accuracy: orders needing no staff correction
  • Time to order compared with the counter
  • Hand-off rate: share of orders that fall back to staff

09Key learnings

  • With voice AI, conversation design is the product. Most of the quality came from rules about when to stop talking and listen.
  • Grounding beats cleverness. An agent that can't invent a price is one a restaurant can trust.
CASE STUDY · INDEPENDENT PRODUCT PROJECT

Hospital Appointment Booking

A booking system for clinic staff. Doctors set their availability, the app works out free slots, staff book from a calendar and patients get a WhatsApp confirmation.

2roles: admin, doctorWeekly rules+ blocked timeWhatsAppconfirmationsTestedtenant isolation
SetDoctor sets weekly hours
→
BlockLeave or blocked slots
→
CalculateOnly free slots shown
→
BookBook, reschedule, cancel
→
ConfirmWhatsApp to patient

01Business context

Many clinics still run appointments on paper registers, phone calls and spreadsheets. As doctors and patients increase, the schedule becomes the clinic's main point of failure.

02Customer problem

  • Staff can't see true availability, so they double-book or turn patients away.
  • Doctors' leave and changes don't reach the front desk in time.
  • Patients leave the call unsure whether they're actually booked.

03My role

Independent product project: I mapped the booking workflow, defined roles and scope, and built and deployed the app.

04Discovery

Mapped the journey from both sides, the person booking and the doctor being booked. The core insight: availability isn't one schedule. It's a weekly pattern plus exceptions, so the product had to model both.

05Prioritisation

MUSTWeekly availability, blocked slots, correct free-slot calculation, book, reschedule and cancel
SHOULDWhatsApp confirmation to patients, admin view of all appointments
LATERPatient self-booking portal, reminders before the visit

06Product decisions

  • Staff-first, not patient-first. Fix the clinic's source of truth before opening booking to the public.
  • Availability as rules + exceptions. Weekly hours with slot lengths, plus blocked slots or whole days off. The app offers only slots that are free, not blocked, in the future and not already taken.
  • WhatsApp over email or SMS. It's where patients in India actually read messages. It's optional, so the clinic still works without it.
  • Strict privacy between doctors. A doctor can never see or change another doctor's appointments, and automated tests enforce it.

07Execution

Built in Ruby on Rails with PostgreSQL. A dedicated slot-calculation service, Twilio for WhatsApp messages, RSpec tests for booking and access rules, CI on GitHub Actions, and deployment on Render with Docker.

08Success metrics

A working, deployed prototype that rules out double-bookings by design. For a clinic pilot, these are the metrics I'd track:

  • Double-bookings: target zero
  • Time to book an appointment on a call
  • No-show rate before and after WhatsApp confirmations
  • Share of bookings made in the app versus the register

09Key learnings

  • Getting the data model right was the product decision. Once availability was modelled correctly, the interface became simple.
  • In healthcare, trust and privacy are features, so they deserve tests like any other feature.
TEARDOWN · PERSONAL ANALYSIS

GitHub Copilot

An AI coding assistant that started as inline autocomplete and grew into chat and agents that can take on whole tasks. It's a useful lens on what makes AI developer tools work, which is close to my work on Harold.

01What it is

An AI assistant inside the developer's editor and on GitHub: code completions, chat about the codebase, and increasingly agent modes that plan changes, edit several files and open pull requests for review.

02Users & jobs

  • Individual developers: "help me write and understand code faster without leaving my editor."
  • Engineering managers: "raise team output without lowering code quality."
  • Enterprises: "adopt AI safely, with control over data, policy and access."

03Business model

Freemium into paid seats: a free tier to try it, paid individual plans, and business and enterprise plans priced per seat with admin and policy controls. The enterprise tier is where the money is, because the buyer is the organisation rather than the developer.

04Why it's winning

  • Distribution: it sits where developers already work, in their editor and on GitHub, so there's no new habit to form.
  • Context: access to repositories, issues and pull requests gives it context a standalone chatbot doesn't have.
  • Low-friction start: autocomplete delivers value in the first minute with no prompt-writing.

05The core tension

More autonomy means more output, but also more for humans to review. An agent that opens many pull requests can move the bottleneck from writing code to reviewing it. Adoption depends on trust, and trust depends on how easily people can check and correct the agent's work.

06Opportunities I'd pursue

  • Plan-first checkpoints: show the agent's plan for approval before it writes code, catching wrong directions early.
  • Outcome metrics over activity metrics: measure merged, unreverted changes rather than suggestions accepted.
  • Review assistance: summarise risk and test coverage on agent pull requests to cut reviewer load.

07Takeaway

For AI agents, the hard product problem isn't capability, it's control. The winners will make an agent's work easy to inspect, steer and undo.

TEARDOWN · PERSONAL ANALYSIS

Practo

India's best-known platform for finding doctors and booking appointments. It's a two-sided marketplace between patients and clinics, and a natural comparison for my clinic booking project.

01What it is

A patient app and website for discovering doctors and booking appointments, along with online consultations and other health services, plus software for clinics to run their practice.

02Users & jobs

  • Patients: "find a doctor I can trust, near me, available soon, and book without calling."
  • Doctors & clinics: "fill my schedule with the right patients and spend less time on admin."

03Business model & flywheel

Patients use it for free. Revenue comes mainly from the provider side, through clinic software and paid visibility, plus fees on paid services such as consultations. The flywheel: more doctors listed brings more patients searching, which makes listing more valuable to doctors.

04The core tension

A discovery marketplace runs on trust: ratings, reviews and fair ranking. Paid visibility earns money but can make patients wonder whether they're seeing the best doctor or the one that paid. Protecting search credibility while monetising providers is the central trade-off.

05Where booking still breaks

  • Many smaller clinics still run their real schedule offline, so online slots can drift from reality.
  • No-shows hurt clinics directly, and the platform's reminders compete with WhatsApp, where patients actually reply.
  • Walk-in clinics need queue status ("you're 4th, about 20 minutes") more than fixed time slots.

06Opportunities I'd pursue

  • WhatsApp-first confirmations and rescheduling to cut no-shows, especially in smaller cities.
  • Live queue status for walk-in clinics.
  • Lightweight clinic tools that keep the clinic's own calendar the single source of truth, so online availability is always correct.

07Takeaway

Healthcare marketplaces win on trust and accuracy, not features. If the slot shown isn't real, nothing else matters. That's why I made availability the core of my own booking app.
